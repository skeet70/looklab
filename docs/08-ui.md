# 08 — UI

Browsing, comparing, matching, verification and previews are anonymous and upload nothing. Contributing needs a pseudonymous passkey, which the UI presents as signing in (§4.1). Seeders, moderators and admins should use hardware-backed passkeys or security keys.

## 0. Audiences and principles

### 0.1 Audiences

| Audience | Arrives wanting | Sees first | Goes deeper into |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Film-sim and Look enthusiasts** | "Which look is this, and how close can I get?" | Looks on sample photos, character summaries, closeness labels | Measurements, provenance, versions |
| **Family and dad-cam photographers** | "Can I get that color without a new camera?" | Camera and look pickers, settings in menu terms | What won't match, card and editing-app options |
| **Street photographers** | "How does this look hold up in mixed light?" | The compare viewer on real scenes, including the difference view | Region maps, uncertainty, checks with their own shots |
| **Former film users** | "Can my digital camera render like the film I used?" | Built-in and custom looks on photos | Origin, character tags, evidence |
| **Returning photographers** who left large kits and heavy post | "Can a small camera give me an analogue look without the kit and the editing?" | A camera, a look and settings on one screen | Simpler apply paths, card downloads, saved recipes |
| **Community helpers** from the firmware-hacking scene, without a technical background | "How can I help?" | What helping takes (time, files), the privacy promise, a drop zone | Their contributions' status and impact, wanted shots |
| **Firmware developers** who want their firmware to load looklab looks | "What do I implement?" | The card format in three steps, downloads, a card inspector | The spec, conformance vectors, per-firmware verification |
| **Moderators** (and seeders, admins) | "What needs a decision?" | The review queue, after signing in | Evidence, the audit log |

These five photographer groups are the default audience. Every page that isn't clearly for another audience is written for them. Developer, community and moderation pages are reachable from the footer and the account menu, not the main navigation.

### 0.2 Principles

1. **Pictures first.** A look is shown on photos before any number. Every look, comparison and match result has an image view.
2. **Three depths.** Each page has a *glance* level (an image and one sentence), an *engage* level (plain-language breakdown, qualitative closeness) and an *inspect* level (plots, numbers with uncertainty, provenance, versions, hashes). Inspect content sits behind a "Measurements" tab or a "Technical details" disclosure and is never the first thing on a page.
3. **Audience-scoped navigation.** The header carries photographer and contributor tasks only (§1.2).
4. **Familiar identity.** Passkeys appear as "Sign in" and "Create a profile". Words like credential, assertion, token, tier and proof-of-work stay out of the glance and engage levels (§0.3).
5. **Neutral surround.** UI chrome is achromatic, so it doesn't bias color judgment (§7.1).
6. **Honest estimates.** Previews are model renders, labeled as such. Early data is badged. Closeness labels widen when uncertainty is high (§0.4). A camera's model is described as measured only where a scoped assessment passed; elsewhere the page says experimental and names what is missing.
7. **Plain purpose.** Each route says what you can do there in photographer language: choose a camera and look, compare two looks, get settings, share photos, or implement the card format. No social-media pitch, superlatives or repeated motivational cadence. These approved lines stay as written: "A spectrum of styles. Your camera."; "Discover what makes other cameras special. Bring it to yours."; "Tuned for your camera"; "Dial it in. Take it out into the world."; "We extract the aesthetic"; "Shared samples".

### 0.4 Closeness scale

Glance and engage levels show one of five ordered labels for a ΔE₍UCS₎ median: Indistinguishable, Nearly identical, Close, Noticeable difference, Different look. The ΔE boundaries between them belong to the color pipeline ([10 Q23](10-open-questions.md)) and live in one typed function that every page uses.

When the 90% band spans two labels, both are shown ("Close to nearly identical"). When confidence is low (thin control-response model, early-estimate look), the label carries "early estimate". The number, its band and p95 are one hover or tap away. This is how "show uncertainty wherever a number appears" (§7.3) applies at the glance level.

### 1.2 Header, account menu and footer

- **Header:** logo, **Looks**, **Compare**, **Get the look**, **Contribute**, and on the right **Sign in** or the account menu. On narrow screens the four links collapse into a menu button; Sign in stays visible.
- **Account menu** (signed in): the display name (or "Your profile"), Your contributions, **Review** with a pending count when available without an extra assertion (moderators, seeders and admins reach only the review actions their role permits, §6), Sign out, Sign out everywhere. Your proposals appears only when a profile-scoped proposal list is available.
- **Account trigger:** a compact round initial (or neutral person icon), one-line ellipsized name and chevron. Below 480 px only the initial or icon and chevron remain visible; the full profile name remains its accessible label. The popup aligns to the trigger's right edge. Enter or Space opens it; Escape closes it and returns focus to the trigger.
- **Footer:** Cameras, For developers, Community, How it works, Privacy, Source (AGPL-3.0-or-later). Integrity statements ("every artifact is checked against its version manifest") move to How it works and to the inspect level of the page that uses the artifact.
- **Preview build** (no server): a slim dismissible notice, "Demo with sample data. Sign-in and sharing are turned off.", and no Sign in button.

## 2. Public pages

The Looks introduction is "Discover what makes other cameras special. Bring it to yours." Match uses "Tuned for your camera" and "Dial it in. Take it out into the world." Contribute uses "We extract the aesthetic" and "Shared samples"; the payload inspector names the actual color samples and metadata.

### 2.1 Home

- **Collecting:** when no public looks are published, explain the collection stage in plain words: a look appears once enough shared RAW and straight-from-camera JPEG pairs have been measured and checked, and photos from more shoots let the settings it gives you be checked too. Link to contributing, to the "Where things stand" section on wanted shots, and to how looks are made. Hide the preview, picker and gallery; the collecting notice replaces the separate help band. Home, Looks, Compare and Match render the same shared notice for this state.
- **Hero:** a sample photo in the compare viewer (§7.2), split between the colorimetric reference on the left (labeled "Neutral") and the chip-selected look on the right (initially the first public look). The reference preview LUT is hash-listed in the site index, not a named sample look. Older indexes without that artifact retain the two-look preview. Keep the neutral frame and look chips under it to switch the right side. The caption stays "Simulated". The home hero omits the view-mode and surround buttons; other viewers retain them. Kicker: "A spectrum of styles. Your camera." Headline: "See a camera's look on a sample photo, then get it on your camera." No supporting line follows the headline.
- **Picker** (heading "Choose your camera and a look"): "What do you shoot?" (camera combobox, or "Detect from a photo": drop a JPEG, EXIF read locally) then "Which look?" (look gallery popover). Continue goes to `/match` with both filled in.
- **Look gallery** (heading "Every look, on the same photo"): every public look as a card rendered on the same sample photo, with name, origin ("Built into Fujifilm X-T5, X-M1" or the custom look's author) and a one-line character ("warm shadows, soft highlights"). The Looks page header carries the same "Add a custom look" link and splits built-in and custom looks into named sections; with no published custom look, the custom section is an empty state with that link. Published look summaries carry a typed visibility field; test fixtures remain directly addressable for acceptance tests but are excluded from public galleries and hero chips. When every published look is an experimental estimate and none is qualified, the gallery says so above the cards and links to wanted shots instead of presenting them as ordinary published looks. A card for an experimental look states the missing evidence its active request names.
- **Help band:** heading "Where the looks come from", the sentence "Built-in looks are measured from RAW and JPEG pairs that people share.", "About a minute with photos you already have", link to `/contribute`. A second line says "Custom looks are .cube LUTs that people made and share." and links "Add a custom look" to `/community/propose?kind=lut_look`.
- **Developer band:** one line that firmware can load these looks from a card file, and a link to `/developers`.

### 2.2 Look page

**Overview tab (glance, engage):**
- A large viewer showing the look on each sample photo, with a photo strip; "Compare with…" opens `/compare` with this look on one side.
- Name, badge (§0.3), origin, and the **character summary**: the comparison summary (P1.6) against the colorimetric reference Look (identity layers, [02](02-color-pipeline.md)), in plain language, plus up to four character tags.
- A LUT Look superseded by a native Look shows "Superseded by *native look*" under the origin, linking to that Look. It stays listed until a moderator hides or removes it (§6).
- A preview-only version (a LUT whose declared base is not pinned) is shown with its preview and "This version is a preview only. There are no settings to match and no download." instead of "Get this look on your camera"; it is absent from look pickers that feed matching. The page names the declared base and the exact composition the preview LUT applies, including whether that is an identified approximation or the neutral fallback. A settings or download path appears only after a fitted representation passes its checks, and it keeps the base and destination-camera limitations visible. A fitted representation whose computation base is an identified approximation is published with an experimental marker, shown as experimental, and names both the declared base and the computation base; it is not a claim about the declared base's camera controls or firmware. A normal Look link and an ordinary shared recipe or result link resolve the current accepted Look and current camera model when loaded, then recompute the best settings; the loaded calculation keeps the resolved version. A `replay=1` result link replays the exact pinned snapshot and says so.
- A source-limited version whose published support has no probe is shown as experimental (empirical scope, not a publication status): the page carries its version notice, the experimental label with the missing evidence the active request names, and a link to wanted shots. It stays matchable and previewable. Its firmware card is offered and labeled experimental, with the missing evidence that would qualify it and a link to wanted shots. The page says there is no editing-app export because export validation needs a supported probe.
- **Get this look on your camera:** a camera picker that opens the match result for that camera; "Custom firmware" and "Editing app" links when cards or exports exist.
- **Camera realizations:** the canonical target is shown separately from its camera realizations. A realization is labeled measured or experimental for the settings it covers, and a higher-version camera-local diagnostic fit is never presented as the family target.
- **Custom looks:** a `lut_defined` look's status comes from its import, frozen approximation and base pin with inherited support and uncertainty ([03 §1](03-data-model.md#1-entities)). The immutable eligibility snapshot supplies the proof and `CurrentTargetEligibility` supplies the current status. It is not shown as experimental merely for lacking a native-family report, and its camera side still shows that realization's own scope.
- **How we know:** "Based on *n* photos from *m* contributors on *k* cameras", the cameras and settings the target's evidence covers, plus weak spots phrased as requests ("We're less sure about deep shadows. Help with a few shots.") linking to wanted shots. Photo counts, mapping confirmation and root admission are provenance, not accuracy claims.
- **Recipes others use** for this look, per camera, with measured closeness.

**Measurements tab (inspect):** the diagnostics of [02 §7](02-color-pipeline.md) with uncertainty bands and hatched unsupported regions; the test charts (gradient, hue wheel, patches).

**History tab (inspect):** version picker, status, `wb_policy`, license and attribution, provenance (cameras and firmware versions, reference sessions with seeder badges, anchor version), changelog, gate results, the artifact hashes with a "matches version manifest" indicator. A notice appears on Overview as well when the version relied on a since-revoked root admission, was produced by a revert, or depended on a qualification assertion that was invalidated; the notice names the affected scope.

### 2.3 Compare

- **Header:** "Compare looks", with "One photo, two looks. Split, toggle, or map the difference."
- **Availability:** resolve selections before requesting artifacts. With no public looks, show the shared collection notice used by Home, Looks and Match. With one public look, compare its two newest versions when available; otherwise offer its look page and contribution links. Unknown looks, versions and scenes offer recovery links rather than an empty identifier or raw 404.
- The selected target takes precedence in unpinned comparisons. Published historical versions are browsable without a current selection and are labeled "Not checked"; browsing does not grant matching or download eligibility.
- **Viewer** (§7.2) filling the width: default view **split** at 50%, plus **side by side**, **toggle** (click or hold to flip) and **difference** (per-pixel ΔE₍UCS₎ between the two renders, sequential color-blind-safe palette, legend in §0.4 labels). State lives in the URL (`a`, `b`, `scene`, `view`).
- Compare links may pin the first and second look versions with `av` and `bv`; switching a look clears that side's version pin. The difference image uses the displayed sRGB renders, sampled to at most 400 × 300 pixels, with a fixed cividis scale from 0 to 10 CAM16-UCS units. It is computed when Difference is selected and cached for the current looks and scene. Its computation yields between row batches and is independent of divider movement. Legend ticks use the shared closeness-band boundaries.
- **Look pickers** on each side as thumbnail chips with a swap button. Either side may be a look, a look version, or (under "More options") a realization on a named camera; a realization side shows its scope and, when experimental, the missing evidence in one line.
- **Scope and invalidation:** each side shows its displayed version's scope label and blockers before the summary, including historical-only versions labeled "Not checked". A version or realization whose applicable qualification or root evidence was withdrawn shows its invalidation notice before its numbers. Compare never implies that an experimental realization is the family target.
- **Photo strip** of sample photos.
- **Summary (engage):** one sentence ("Cold Steel is cooler, with less saturated greens; contrast is nearly identical"), the overall closeness label, and five rows (contrast & brightness, neutral tint, saturation, hue, color brightness), each with a small bar and one sentence.
- When no probes support both looks, or fewer than half the comparison probes are supported, the summary says "Not enough measured data to rate this difference yet". It withholds closeness labels, bars and numerical distributions; model overlays remain available with an explicit prediction-only notice. Zero supported probes are not an indistinguishable result.
- **Show measurements (inspect):** diagnostic overlays, ΔE distribution, region map, layer attribution.
- A caption under the viewer: "Simulated: both looks applied to a sample photo by our model."

### 2.4 Cameras

Heading "Cameras", lede "Pick a camera to see its built-in looks and what the evidence covers." A list of camera cards: make and model, how many looks are measured, and thumbnails of its built-in looks. Filter by make. Measured status is scoped to settings and domains and is stated on the camera page; a card carries no camera-wide verified badge, and counts are not accuracy claims.

Documentation-only cameras are merged into the same list as one card per body, with an explicit human-unconfirmed label, documented/confirmed/contradicted control counts, source count and preparation readiness. Photo thumbnails are optional; without a published sample photo the card and its metadata still render, and only the look strip is omitted. A catalog entry does not imply contribution or matching support.

### 2.5 Camera page

**Overview:**
- Header: make and model, with the lede "Its built-in looks, the evidence behind them, and settings to get one on your camera."
- When this camera has no published public looks, replace the built-in gallery and look picker with a camera-specific collection notice and contribution/how-it-works links.
- **Built-in looks** as a gallery of cards (sample photo render, badge, data volume in words, cross-camera agreement in words).
- **Status:** which settings and domains are measured and which are experimental, with the missing evidence named ("no independent re-render evidence for Color +2"). Counts, mapping confirmation and root admission are provenance, not empirical checks. When no numerically valid, gate-passing realization exists, the page names the blocker for in-camera settings and offers contribution and wanted shots. A valid target card remains downloadable for custom firmware, with target evidence and firmware translation labeled separately.
- **Get any look on this camera:** look picker into the match result.
- **Ways to apply looks:** stock settings, custom firmware that loads cards (listed by firmware id once their renders carry the render tag, §8), editing-app pipelines.
- **Realization notes** in plain language, e.g. "renders about 0.5 EV darker in DR200 than the reference camera".
- **Help with this camera:** its wanted shots; recipes for this camera.

**Technical details (inspect):** mapping status (`fixture-backed` or `provisional`, shown as "Confirmed from a sample file" or "Not yet confirmed from a sample file"), input transform confidence (source, anchoring count, tier), the control schema per firmware range with differences between ranges, capabilities. Fit readiness is separate from the catalog entry, menu mappings and measured status: a body without a published input transform can still be documented, discovered and contribute, and shows that blocker where a match would need it. Preparation readiness alone never enables matching.

**Documented controls and confirmation:** show documentation citations, firmware scope, owner agreement, contradictions and moderator-checked evidence per control, distinguishing documentation from confirmation. Confirmed controls show the agreed options, including corrections, for their reported firmware; a camera-wide confirmed label requires confirmed controls with a common explicit firmware scope. Checks from profiles without a linked photo of the camera count immediately as one group per profile, and linked answers below the three-group quorum also count toward "Owner-reported" one profile per linked identity group: when they agree and none conflict the control shows "Owner-reported" with the reported options and the profile count in words ("reported by 2 profiles"), and when they disagree it shows "Owners disagree" with every option and its count. A correction is shown beside the documented claim, never in place of it. A moderator decision overrides owner reports for its firmware scope; agreeing reports create no review item. Checks received are shown separately from owner agreement, per control and in total, with how many came from profiles not linked to the camera by a photo and how many answer an older control definition. With a live server the counts are fetched after the page loads; until they arrive, or when they cannot be loaded, the page shows no count figures. A serverless preview shows the published snapshot's counts. After a signed confirmation the owner sees how many checks were recorded and whether their profile is eligible to count. The confirmation form asks owners to check selected controls or the complete set, enter their firmware and correct options in typed menu editors; a camera without contribution support accepts a signed confirmation with no photos, and the owner sees the current agreement after submission and can withdraw the declaration. Catalog and definition hashes belong under attestation details. A camera with contribution support also shows the same confirmation form, so an owner can retry a camera confirmation that failed after their photos were accepted. Published manual pages are listed in the authenticated site index and checked against their hash and size before display; when a page no longer matches the cached index, the index is fetched and verified again once before the page is refused.

**Camera changes:** editing a batch's camera discards the previous camera's prefill and marks the batch unchecked until the new camera's published schema is loaded. Processing and sending wait for that check; a transient lookup failure offers **Check camera again** rather than a permanent blocker.

### 2.6 How it works

Route `/about/how-it-works`, eyebrow "Method". The lede is "looklab measures camera looks from shared photos, then gives you settings for your camera or a card file for custom firmware." Header actions: Browse looks (`/looks`), Add a custom look (`/community/propose?kind=lut_look`), Help with photos (`/contribute`). A three-step strip reuses the developer-steps layout: Bring photos, We measure the look, You get settings or a card file.

- **Two kinds of looks:** built-in looks are the film simulations and picture styles a camera ships with, measured from RAW and straight-from-camera (SOOC) JPEG pairs; photos from many cameras separate the look from one sensor. Custom looks are `.cube` LUTs that people made and share, needing no photos of their own except to measure the look they were built on. A LUT made on top of a film simulation depends on that simulation; until measured it can be previewed but has no card file, and at most Experimental settings.
- **What you get:** in-camera settings are the closest menu settings on the camera, which may not reach every part of the look; card files are `.llk` files that custom firmware loads as in-camera looks, reaching more of the pipeline than menu settings; editing-app exports fit apps with a published pipeline. Links to `/match` and `/developers`.
- **Why straight-from-camera JPEGs:** a SOOC JPEG shows how the camera rendered its RAW, while an edited or exported JPEG shows the edit and cannot measure the camera's look. Lightroom, RapidRAW and Capture One presets cannot be added directly: convert the preset to a `.cube` LUT, then add it as a custom look. Global color and tone carry over; masks, vignettes, grain and sharpening do not. The paragraph links to the preset guide at `/community/propose?kind=lut_look#from-a-preset`.
- **Where things stand:** a look publishes once its photos pass review; it shows as Experimental for a camera until independent photo sets from that camera let it be checked for those settings, and more photos widen what the estimate covers. Photo counts describe how much evidence exists, not how accurate the result is. The browser reads photos locally and sends only color samples and the camera settings they were shot with, after review. Link to wanted shots.
- **For firmware projects:** card files are not tied to one camera; the firmware reads the card and translates it into its own pipeline. Card loaders are written by firmware projects and listed on `/developers` once their renders carry the render tag. Link to `/developers`.
- Keeps the "What does close mean?" scale and the "Evidence you can follow" section with the audit log and the technical-details disclosure.

## 3. Match flow ("Get the look")

A three-step wizard with a visible stepper. Each step has a sensible default, so a photographer who arrives with camera and look already chosen lands on the result.

1. **Your camera:** combobox, or detect from a dropped JPEG (EXIF read locally). Changing camera clears its previous settings and firmware selection; Continue and Finish wait for the new camera's settings to load, with an error if loading fails. Firmware is asked only when the camera has more than one firmware range, in menu terms ("Which firmware is on your camera? Not sure? Pick the newest."); it maps to an epoch fuzzily, with a provisional warning.
2. **The look:** look gallery, usable without thumbnails when sample photos cannot load. Optional "What matters most?": balanced (default), skin tones, landscapes, products. With no public look published, the wizard shows the shared collection notice. When public looks exist but none has a matchable version, it says matching is not ready yet and keeps the contribution path instead of calling the catalog empty. A matchable version without a qualified realization for this camera offers experimental matching explicitly once the realization is numerically valid and gate-passing. The in-camera settings path needs that realization; the custom-firmware card path needs a publishable valid target and its card, so a source-limited target with no supported probe offers a card labeled experimental, while a missing camera model blocks settings, not the card download. Otherwise the wizard names the exact blocker for the affected path ([04 §1](04-matching-and-export.md#1-matching-levels)).
3. **How will you apply it?** Cards filtered to the camera: **In-camera settings** (default; any firmware), **Custom firmware** (card file), **Editing app** (an export for an app with a published pipeline), **Other software** (generic LUT). Each card says in one line what you'll get.
4. **Result:**
   - **Viewer:** split between the target look and "your camera at these settings", both on the same sample photo. The realization preview LUT is baked in the match worker from the predicted realization. Render the thumbnail first, then upgrade to the full-size scene without resetting the viewer. A failed full-size load leaves the smaller preview visible with an explanation.
   - **Settings card** ([04 §4](04-matching-and-export.md)), styled as a recipe card: base sim and controls in menu order and wording, including EC/WB shift where useful; exact steps from the camera schema, with half steps shown as decimals such as −1.5 and thirds as fractions such as +1/3 EV; the closeness label (§0.4); "What won't match exactly" lists only regions at least in the Close category, in plain words, or says "Nothing noticeable is predicted to differ." Numbers (median, p95, band) under "Details". The result names any native sim the bundle did not rank and why ("Not considered: Velvia / Vivid (no published response)."). The card states whether the result is measured or experimental and, when experimental, what is missing. The result pins its bundle revision, selection mode and the full applicable report set for the target and every ranked sim; replay reproduces that recorded context and queries current applicability separately.
   - **Actions:** Copy as text, Share link (the permalink), Save to my profile (signs in or creates a profile, §4.1), **Check with your shots** (the verification loop of [04 §5](04-matching-and-export.md), inline: drop the JPEGs you shot at these settings, see measured vs predicted closeness and the improved suggestion).
- **Card file:** download of the version's `card.llk`, checked against the manifest, with the 8.3 file name to use, install steps in plain words, and a pointer to the firmware list. Verification is in-camera renders tagged by the firmware ([04 §8.4](04-matching-and-export.md)). Download provenance names the target and card evidence and says when firmware translation is unverified; it does not invent a pinned camera realization when the card path used none. The bytes are the version's unchanged card. A card for an experimental target is labeled experimental in plain words, with the missing independent captures that would qualify it and a link to wanted shots.
   - **Export:** download, what it applies to, unsupported-region note; validation report (including per-CCT results for single-illuminant profiles) under "Details". An export from an experimental realization is labeled experimental in its report and download metadata. An experimental target with no supported probe offers no export because export validation needs a supported probe.
5. **Permalinks** pin every model version used ([04 §4](04-matching-and-export.md)), so a result always reproduces. A link also records its bundle revision, selection mode and the full applicable report set for the target and every ranked sim; replay reproduces that context and shows an invalidation notice when a pinned report is no longer current. An experimental link with no applicable report replays as experimental and not checked, and a legacy link without recorded selection context replays as not checked.

A non-identity local calibration overlay labels the resulting settings "Experimental: locally adjusted, not independently assessed", even when the pinned baseline was qualified. The next share link records that mode; baseline qualification remains visible separately ([04 §4](04-matching-and-export.md#4-settings-card-and-permalinks)).

The sample-data build publishes synthetic camera response models for each sample look and camera, labeled as demonstrations and marked experimental and not checked on the result. Saved recipes are scoped to the signed-in profile in this browser; a share link transfers a recipe to another device. Local verification needs each JPEG's original RAW reference to measure colors. Without paired RAW files, the inline section accepts the JPEGs and asks for their originals. Editing-app cards appear only when a compatible editing-app pipeline is published. The generic software download uses shaped scene-linear Rec.2020 input and encoded sRGB output, not finished JPEG input; its Details state the required conversion and the absence of independent pipeline validation.

## 4. Contribute, sign in and propose

### 4.1 Sign in and your profile

A contributor profile is a passkey plus optional display name. There's no email or password.

- **Sign in dialog** (header button, or any action that needs a profile): title "Sign in to looklab"; one line, "No email or password. Your device keeps a passkey that proves your contributions are yours."; a **Continue with passkey** button (a discoverable-credential assertion); "New here? **Create a profile**" (registration with an optional display name, labeled "Shown next to your public credits. Leave blank to stay anonymous."); a "How this works" link to `/help/passkeys`. WebAuthn reports "no passkey" and "cancelled" as the same `NotAllowedError`, so a failed assertion never switches to registration on its own.
- **Help navigation:** "How this works" closes the sign-in dialog before opening the help page.
- **User verification:** passkey registration and every signed server action require verification by the authenticator.
- **No detected platform passkey store** (`isUserVerifyingPlatformAuthenticatorAvailable()` is false): the dialog and contribution notice say, "No built-in passkey store was detected. Use a password manager, your phone, or a security key."
- **Errors** read as next steps: a cancelled prompt returns to the dialog with "Try again"; an unknown passkey says "We don't recognise that passkey. Create a profile instead?"
- **Sessions:** signing in issues a status token for this browser ([06 §4](06-privacy-and-payload.md)), kept in IndexedDB. Opening `/status/:token` stores that token the same way, so a status link works as a sign-in link. **Sign out** forgets the token locally; **Sign out everywhere** revokes every token for the profile.
- **Roles:** the sign-in response tells the signed-in contributor their own roles, so the account menu can show Review (§6). Role-gated actions require their own assertions (§6).
- **Your contributions** (`/me`): one card per signed send, identified by its date, camera, photo count and built-in looks or recipes. The card summarizes use, waiting states and exclusion reasons, with one signed action to withdraw its photos. It also shows when a scoped model claim that used the contribution was invalidated, with the reason and the evidence that would restore it. Legacy records without a send identity are labeled **Earlier activity** and grouped by UTC day and camera. **Technical details** contains contribution/image IDs, per-image status, timestamps and RMS measurements in a horizontally scrollable table with unwrapped identifiers. A **Private status link** button opens a dialog with the full capability URL, a **Copy link** action and the warning that anyone given the link can read the contributions; the dialog opens over `/me` and closing it leaves the page and sign-in untouched. The warning appears only in that dialog. **Subscribe with RSS** opens the feed itself (`application/rss+xml`), not the SPA status route.
- **Private-link copying:** if clipboard access is denied, select the full readonly URL and announce that it is selected for manual copying.
- **Startup sign-in:** `/me` shows loading while a saved sign-in is being restored. Explicit sign-in, private-link activation and sign-out take precedence over startup restoration. The signed-in page does not repeat sign-in instructions.
- Deferred monochrome submissions show "received, awaiting monochrome analysis", their retention deadline and a withdrawal action. They show no consistency result, scientific credit or promise of a published Look.

### 4.2 Contribute ("Help build looks")

**Landing (`/contribute`):**
- Eyebrow "What helping takes", heading "Help build looks". The privacy promise in plain words comes before anything else: "Your photos never leave this device. We extract the aesthetic, and you see exactly what's sent before it goes." A following sentence says shared samples are how a look is measured.

Every supported camera can contribute; there is no camera allowlist and no camera-wide approval to wait for. A contribution can be accepted while its camera's model stays experimental for settings it has not checked ([07 §5](07-contribution.md#5-contributor-feedback)).
- Contribution readiness uses the published camera/control schema and local RAW decoder and matrix checks. A missing server input prior does not hide contribution actions or block local preparation. Server fitting and stock-settings matching retain their resource requirements.
- **Ways to help** as cards with time and impact, no rung numbers ([07 §1](07-contribution.md)): Share photos you already have (about a minute), Take a few wanted shots (5 to 10 minutes, links to `/contribute/wanted`), Re-render a few RAWs in camera (about 2 minutes), Photograph a pattern on your phone (about 3 minutes), Photograph a gradient (about 2 minutes), Photograph a color chart (about 5 minutes). Impact is shown as low, medium or high, never as a weight.
- Every way-to-help card is a button opening the shared Dialog with its time, impact and numbered capture steps from [07 §3](07-contribution.md). Escape closes it and returns focus to the card. Its primary action opens the existing file picker and continues at the drop step without changing contribution-kind detection. The wanted-shots walkthrough embeds the top three available requests using the wanted-shot cards, plus “See all wanted shots”. The phone-pattern walkthrough opens the public fixture pattern at `/pattern/:seed` in a new tab and provides a QR code of its absolute URL plus a selectable text URL. The URL respects the site's deployment prefix. The pattern fills the screen without site chrome, and tapping it requests fullscreen where supported.
- A styled **drop zone** for files or a folder, with the prompt centered on the file/folder buttons' centerline.
- Drafts contain the selected files, settings, prepared samples and local verification seeds on this device only. File bytes are written once under separate IndexedDB keys, and all draft writes are serialized. Settings backed by prepared samples are debounced by 300 ms; settings without prepared samples are flushed immediately and atomically delete stale preparation. Changing B&W consent invalidates preparation and disables Review and Send until processing completes again, including after draft restoration; a restored draft keeps its saved partial B&W selection partial, and a new drop starts unchecked. Withdrawing B&W consent writes a synchronous guard before the draft save; a reload while that save is pending restores B&W as unselected and discards the prepared samples, and the guard clears only after the save that deletes that preparation commits. If the browser cannot read or write the guard, B&W renders are left out, their prepared samples are discarded, and a notice says so. A completed upload clears its draft and locks backward navigation to prevent resending the batch. The landing explains local storage and asks users to keep their original files.
- Prepared metadata must survive draft restoration bit for bit. The contribution e2e flow processes files, restores the draft, uploads, then runs `verify-payload` against the contribution's named community snapshot and the served source-built prep module.

**Flow** (a stepper; leaving keeps what's done; drafts persist locally in IndexedDB for resuming, and the UI doesn't depend on drafts surviving eviction):
1. **Drop files or a folder.** Local pairing, metadata read, grouping, coverage preselection. Shows pairs found, grouped as cards by camera and look, and unpaired or excluded files with plain reasons. Recognized B&W renders can enter the deferred-monochrome path, including a RAW whose only JPEGs are B&W ([10 Q26](10-open-questions.md)).
2. **Check the groups:** native vs. recipe (with target); settings from makernotes, editable where unreadable, in menu terms. Each setting renders by its schema type: an enum as a dropdown of the camera's values, a boolean as a Choose/Off/On select, and an integer or decimal as a number input showing the real value with the schema's range and step, and the schema default as a placeholder until a value is entered; clearing a number that came from the files restores its saved value, while clearing an unreadable number removes it; the stored `key=step` lines and the technical editor stay available. One unchecked **Contribute all B&W renders in this selection for monochrome model development** selection, above the per-batch detail, applies to every B&W batch in the selection and states how many B&W renders are included and how many are left out; a restored partial draft shows it indeterminate and changes nothing until clicked, and colour batches are unaffected. The selected batch's editor is headed with that batch's look and render count and says its edits apply only to that batch's renders, so per-batch settings stay distinct from the drop-level B&W selection. The B&W selection names the purpose (developing and validating monochrome response, toning and uncertainty), the 180-day retention limit, withdrawal rights and that submission may never produce a published Look. Without it, B&W renders are left out. Applicable filter, WC/MG toning and grain settings require reproduced values or explicit declarations. Preserve the original native sim and show normalized family/filter separately. Process stays disabled while an included batch is missing a value for an applicable control the files did not reproduce; the step lists each batch and setting, and each name selects the batch and focuses its field. A draft restored with missing values opens on Check groups.
3. **Processing on this device,** with progress and the live coverage map captioned "What your renders add".
4. **Review what's sent:** a one-line summary ("1,204 samples from 37 colour renders, 380 KB. No pixels, no location.") that counts prepared colour and B&W renders separately; included B&W renders are labeled as monochrome model development for up to 180 days, not colour fitting, and unprepared or unselected renders appear in the explicit "Files left out" list with their reasons. It expands into the full payload inspector ([06 §7](06-privacy-and-payload.md)).
5. **Send:** signs in or creates a profile if needed (§4.1), then **one passkey gesture for the whole send**, however many batches it holds. The browser signs one versioned send manifest listing every batch's hash and optional camera-confirmation hashes. Proof-of-work shows as "Preparing upload (this keeps spam out)"; upload progress counts photos rather than signed batches. The **Export seed file (SENSITIVE)** button sits next to the send button; it is local only and never uploaded ([06 §7](06-privacy-and-payload.md)).
6. **Done:** thanks, the photo and camera counts, a link to Your contributions, and the next way to help, each skippable. Summarize colour submissions separately from B&W measurements "received, awaiting monochrome analysis"; neither label implies a completed consistency check. Acceptance does not imply that the camera's model is measured for every setting; scoped status arrives with the feedback. Back/Next is hidden after completion. Feedback arrives asynchronously on `/me`.
   - Report optional confirmation failures separately from accepted photos. A successful photo send is not presented as lost when its confirmation needs another attempt.

**Wanted shots (`/contribute/wanted`):** bounties and evidence requests as cards: what to shoot in plain words, which look and camera it helps, and a capture tip ([07 §3](07-contribution.md)). Show active prerequisite requests even when there are no bounties or fitted candidates. Camera and simulation names are primary; subject and report identifiers are technical details. Requests come from automatic missing-evidence detection, not a moderator promotion decision.
- **Where things stand:** above the cards, one entry per camera in the site index with its published built-in looks, its open evidence requests and its bounties. An evidence request names its Look by the display name and separates that label from the request text; a raw setting key is never shown at this level. It shows the observed independent groups only when the request publishes that count; otherwise it shows the request text alone. A request or bounty without a body is listed once above the cameras under "Any camera with these looks can help", not repeated under each camera. An unpublished Look shows its camera menu name, never a raw setting key or a lowercase slug. Counts are provenance, not accuracy (§2.4), and no camera carries a verified badge. When nothing is open, the section says so. The collecting notice of §2.1 links here.
- Wanted-shot impact uses expected variance reduction: below 0.01 is low, 0.01 to below 0.1 is medium, and 0.1 or above is high. These are qualitative UI cut points, not contribution weights.
- Evidence-request cards describe the missing observations and their capture requirements. Expected-impact labels belong to bounties with measured variance-reduction estimates.

### 4.3 Propose

`/community/propose` is photographer-friendly. The page starts from one of five kinds; epoch splits, look splits and gate overrides come from workers and moderators and are not in the public picker. The picker groups them as Your look (custom look), Your camera (camera, film simulation, controls) and For developers (editing-app pipeline). The heading and lede follow the selected kind: a custom look says "Add a custom look" and explains that a moderator checks the license and credit, then approves the import, and that approval alone does not publish the look; camera kinds keep "Tell us what your camera can do". The picker says where an approved result appears: cameras under Cameras, custom looks on Looks, in Compare and in Get the look, editing-app pipelines on For developers → Editing apps and in the Editing app result.

- **One state per kind.** Body, mapping, control change, custom look and editing-app pipeline each own their draft. Switching kind clears the evidence selection and pending donation confirmation; each kind keeps its own form state.
- **Canonical payload.** Structured proposals are built by the browser with the same `looklab-schema` WASM validator CI uses, including `encode_proposal` and `apply_control_changes`. An editing-app pipeline keeps its file text as opaque source inside the validated proposal. A first schema keeps its complete document as opaque source inside the validated `SchemaChange` proposal. The preview and the submitted payload are that output, not a hand-written document. A form error appears in the form, and Submit reports the computed error rather than an empty payload.
- **Menu terms first.** Every form asks in camera-menu language, for example "adds Reala Ace to X-T5 firmware 2.0+" or "Highlight tone goes −2 to +4 in half steps". YAML is the Advanced view of the computed payload, and editing it replaces the form output until reset.
- **Links come from human names.** Make and model produce `/cameras/{make}/{model}`; a look name produces `/looks/{slug}`; a film-simulation name produces its sim key and Look link. There are no slug fields. An empty make, model, look name or film-simulation name is reported against that field rather than as an identifier error. Before submit the form checks the site index: an existing camera link, or a Look link with published versions, blocks and names the existing entry; a server conflict from a concurrent submission is reported as an error rather than replacing the form.
- **Camera model aliases.** Enter one full EXIF model name per line. Spaces inside an alias are preserved.
- **Camera and mapping evidence.** Fixture donation appears only for a camera proposal whose kind is Camera, and for a mapping. A software body, a custom look, an editing-app pipeline and a control change show no donation control. A camera marked "Confirmed from a sample file" requires at least one ticked file; every donation is an explicit per-file opt-in. The evidence section identifies the camera once both its make and model are available. Before anything leaves, a confirmation lists the exact files with sizes and sha256; only ticked files are uploaded, and they stay private until a moderator approves the proposal. Changing the camera, proposal or file selection invalidates that confirmation, and a changed camera selection, kind or evidence-file selection discards in-flight evidence reads. Mappings without a fixture ship as `provisional`.
- **Camera edit starts from the published schema.** The form selects an existing camera, loads its published control schema, and asks for a firmware range. Controls are an explicit multi-select; each selected control gets its own editor initialised from the published definition, and only changed fields enter the diff. Adding a change again replaces the queued diff for that control in that firmware range, so a field returned to its published value is not submitted. Untouched controls and optional definitions stay as published. A registered camera without a schema has an explicit first-schema section: select the camera, paste or load the complete validated schema, and submit the same signed `SchemaChange` payload. The form does not reuse another camera's mappings. "Add a control this camera does not list" is a separate empty draft; it never inherits the selected control's values and adds `tolerated` only when written. A lookup is fenced: a slow earlier camera response cannot overwrite a later selection.
- **Film-simulation suggestions.** Typing a name that already exists on the same camera offers each match, including the same key in different firmware ranges, identified by epoch and sim key. "Correct when an existing simulation became available" keeps the exact Look link, makernote tag and value, and monochrome flag from the source definition; only the firmware range changes. Choosing the source range again is rejected as a no-op.
- **Read the menu value from files.** Several labeled original camera files infer the makernote tag/value mapping locally; the photos stay on the device.
- **Custom looks are upload-first.** The `.cube` file is chosen first, parsed and summarised locally, then the name, attribution and license follow. Choosing another `.cube` replaces the previous attachment and resets the Advanced payload to it. An Advanced payload that names different LUT bytes is rejected before submission. The ownership declaration starts unticked; authors explicitly claim their own work or provide a redistribution license. The file is uploaded with the signed proposal and stays private until approval. The form asks what the LUT expects in photographer terms: "My LUT expects F-Log2 (F-Log2 / F-Gamut)" for scene-referred input, or "My LUT goes on top of a finished look (a film simulation, a camera's standard look, or an app's default)" for display-referred input. The base list contains the neutral baseline and published or rooted Look versions from the site index, including labelled source-only previews that cannot be matched; a camera base uses the selected camera's published film-simulation menu, never a typed sim key, and a family base names the maker and rendering. A Look link that already has published versions is rejected before submit, and the fixed neutral-baseline link is reserved. A terminal import failure releases its link so a corrected proposal can reuse the name. Internal keys stay in the Advanced payload.
- **Base outcome.** Beside the base choice, the form previews what that base makes available from the same typed base status the worker uses; a non-matchable preview base is treated as unmeasured because import refuses it for matching. A pinned base previews, offers in-camera settings once the destination camera's model is ready, and offers a card file. The neutral baseline adds that it is measured on a neutral rendering, not the app's default. An identified but unmeasured base previews now and offers settings and a card once that base is measured, with links to wanted shots and contributing. An incomplete or inconsistent base names the missing or conflicting field, and a base whose camera schema has not loaded says which data is needed. A LUT made on top of a film simulation depends on that simulation until it is measured.
- **Starting from a preset.** A `#from-a-preset` disclosure on the custom-look form says presets from Lightroom, RapidRAW or Capture One can't be added directly. It explains making a `.cube` by applying the preset to a HALD identity image in the editor and converting the result, notes that RapidRAW can export an edit as a `.cube` directly, including from its command line, that global color and tone carry over while masks, vignettes, grain and sharpening don't, and that a preset built on a film simulation or app default needs that base. It says to turn off masks, vignette, grain, sharpening and lens corrections before exporting and to export the HALD at full size as a 16-bit TIFF or PNG with no resizing, because baked spatial effects corrupt the grid. It names no third-party converter sites. RapidRAW's direct `.cube` export is documented in its README, and it can also apply `.cube` and HALD LUTs.
- **Editing-app pipelines are upload-first.** A pipeline is a looklab YAML definition of a software pipeline. The YAML is chosen first and validated locally; the form shows its id and name. Choosing another file replaces the previous text and resets the Advanced payload to it. An explanation and format link are available before file selection. The full file text is the opaque source inside the validated proposal. Published pipelines appear on For developers → Editing apps and enable the Editing app result in Get the look. Firmware authors implement the card format instead of submitting a pipeline.
- **Confirmations are evidence, not votes.** Camera-body, control-schema and film-simulation mapping proposals offer "My camera shows this menu too" only while they are open (awaiting moderation or needing information). The confirmation attaches the confirmer's parsed, whitelisted tag values from their own file without uploading the photo. Editing-app (software) bodies, custom looks, editing-app pipelines, worker-only model decisions and closed proposals show no camera-menu confirmation form; their evidence is listed without it. Moderators decide.
- **Proposal detail is honest about import.** An approved custom look shows the import state: queued, running, failed with the reason, or published with the Look link and version. Approval alone is never presented as publication; a failed import says that no Look version exists.
- Proposals are rate-limited per credential and require proof-of-work.
- **Discussion** is limited to the proposer and moderators ("request info"). There's no open forum.

## 5. Sample photos

- **Photo scenes:** 4 to 6 photographs decoded from CC0 RAW files (e.g. raw.pixls.us), each with a mix of foliage, sky, neutrals, saturated objects or skin-like tones. No identifiable people until model-released owner shots exist ([10 Q14](10-open-questions.md)). Each records its source URL, license and source sha256 next to the scene.
- **Processing:** decoded by `looklab-raw`, mapped to scene-linear XYZ with the file's color matrix, white-balanced as shot and adapted to D65, exposure normalized so the scene's median luminance is near 0.18, downscaled to at most 1600 px on the long edge. Stored as 16-bit RGB PNG of the preview LUT's shaped scene-linear Rec.2020 coordinates, quantized to 12 bits, plus a thumbnail variant of at most 400 px for cards. These PNGs are LUT coordinates, not display images.
- **Test charts** (a neutral and hue gradient, a hue wheel, and skin/foliage/sky patches) stay in the Measurements tab and the compare photo strip's "Test charts" group.
- The `site_data` example and the production worker publish the same scenes; the worker reads the prepared photo directory under its data tree ([ops/deployment](ops/deployment.md)). Each look version publishes a 33³ preview LUT that the browser applies, so a look switch costs only the LUT application: < 200 ms at viewer size *(tentative)*, with thumbnails rendered lazily as cards scroll into view.
- **Preview gamut:** LUT baking contracts scene chromaticities outside the CAM16 domain toward D65 at fixed nonnegative luminance. Appearance targets outside sRGB lose chroma at fixed CAM16-UCS lightness and hue, with lightness bounded by the displayable neutral range ([04 §7](04-matching-and-export.md#7-baking)). Stored LUT channels are finite and within `[0, 1]`.
- **Labels:** every rendered image carries "Simulated" in its caption or alt text. The glance level never presents a render as a camera's own JPEG.
- **Later:** 8 to 12 project-owned scenes with model releases (skin in several tones, foliage, sky, neutral interiors, saturated products, a night or high-DR scene) replace the CC0 set.

Approval refreshes intake schemas and queues catalog publication independently of Look fitting. Interrupted publication resumes from its recorded snapshot version and content hash.

## 7. General UI notes

### 7.1 Visual system

- **Achromatic chrome:** every background, surface, border and text color has R = G = B. Dark and light themes follow `prefers-color-scheme`. One accent color marks interactive controls and never borders or surrounds an image.
- **Image surround:** viewers sit on a mid-gray surround (sRGB 119, about 18% reflectance) by default, switchable to black or white.
- **Type:** system UI font stack; prose at most about 70 characters per line; numbers in tabular figures.
- **Layout:** images get the width. Pages lead with the image or the primary action; text follows. The header, main content and footer share one horizontal content edge at every width. Text-led pages keep their reading widths but start at that same edge; reading-width limits belong inside the shared main element. Reserve scrollbar space so short and long pages do not shift that edge during navigation.
- **Plots** keep their own light canvas and the Okabe-Ito palette (§7.3).

### 7.2 Components

Shared Leptos components in `looklab-web`, used by every page instead of page-local markup:

| Component | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Viewer | Renders one or two LUTs on a scene to canvas. Views: single, split (draggable divider; `role="slider"` with arrow-key, Home/End and touch support), side by side, toggle, difference. Surround switch. Caption with "Simulated". |
| Look card / look picker | Thumbnail render, name, origin, badge. The picker is a searchable popover gallery. |
| Camera picker | Combobox over cameras with make grouping, plus "Detect from a photo". |
| Closeness | The §0.4 label, with median, p95 and band on hover, focus or tap. |
| Stepper | An ordered list of wizard steps with current, complete and upcoming states, each completed step's one-line summary, and back/next until the flow is finished. |
| Disclosure, tabs | Glance/engage/inspect separation. |
| Dialog | Sign in, confirmations. Focus-trapped, Escape closes. |
| Drop zone | Files or a folder, drag and drop, keyboard reachable buttons. Replaces raw file inputs. |
| Badge, notice, empty state | Status words from §0.3; empty states say what would fill them and how to help. The no-published-Looks notice is one shared view used by Home, Looks, Compare and Match. Status and error messages wrap long identifiers within the available width. |

Photo previews start with the hash-checked scene thumbnail, then replace it with the full scene after the first image has painted. Home waits for both preview LUTs before starting the full-scene upgrade and gallery enrichment. Look and Compare defer their photo strips until the hero image has painted and preserve their viewer state during the full-scene upgrade; a failed upgrade leaves the thumbnail visible. Compare loads Look documents and computes its measurements after the initial image paint. The Look page mounts its measurements and test charts only when Measurements is first opened, preserving them on later tab changes. A pinned match result shows the target look while its worker calculates the camera settings, then displays the target and predicted camera render together.

Browsing fetches only the Look document and preview LUT, not matching models, covariance files or card downloads. Verified scene preparation is cached across routes within the current document, with a two-million-pixel cache limit. Full-size PNG decoding and `PreparedScene` construction run in a separate import-free scene worker using the existing scalar Rust functions. Its response preserves each interpolation corner and f32 weight bit pattern; the main thread validates the response against the verified scene dimensions before rendering. Thumbnails stay on the main thread. Browser image decoding is not used for these RGB16 scientific scene assets. Prep and match worker build settings are unchanged.

The release targets are below 2 s from empty-cache localhost navigation to the first photo on Home, Look, Compare and pinned Match, and below 200 ms for LUT application when switching looks. `site/scripts/first-photo.mjs` records navigation, resource and first-photo timestamps, plus main-thread long tasks during the full-size upgrade. `looklab.scene.decode` and `looklab.scene.prepare` measure local thumbnail preparation, `looklab.scene.receive` measures transferred sample validation, and `looklab.lut.apply` and `looklab.canvas.paint` separate rendering stages. `site/scripts/scene-worker-check.mjs` compares prepared samples and rendered RGBA byte for byte against the UI-compiled main-thread Rust reference on all nine scenes. These budgets are targets, not guarantees for every device or server.

### 7.3 Uncertainty, accessibility, mobile

- Show uncertainty wherever a number appears. At the glance and engage levels, that's the closeness label of §0.4.
- Plain language first; details one click away.
- Accessible plots: color-blind-safe palettes, labels and patterns, never color alone.
- Every control is keyboard reachable with a visible focus ring. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Mobile: browsing, look pages, the compare viewer (touch drag), the settings card and the pattern page must work well. Large-folder processing may want a desktop, but should degrade gracefully.

### 7.4 Automatic error visibility

Server-backed builds install `site/errors.js` before WASM boot. It reports console errors, uncaught exceptions, unhandled rejections and application resource failures, including worker failures, without asking the visitor to assemble a bug report. Normal console output and browser error handling remain available.

Only failure categories, route categories, build identity and application code locations leave the browser. Messages, console arguments, photo data and private URLs stay local. The privacy page discloses the channel and retention; [06 §1.1](06-privacy-and-payload.md#11-automatic-error-diagnostics) defines the whitelist. Reporting is bounded, deduplicated and best-effort; a reporting failure changes no application result and is not reported recursively. Serverless previews send nothing.

## 8. Developers

**Firmware (`/developers`):**
- "Make your firmware load looklab looks" in three steps: read a card file, translate it into your pipeline at startup, write the render tag into every JPEG ([04 §8](04-matching-and-export.md)).
- **Resources:** the card format spec, the conformance vectors (`data/card/conformance-v1/` published as `data/developers/conformance-v1.zip`, with its path and sha256 in `data/developers/conformance-v1.json`), the C99 example translator (`tools/card-translator-c99`), and `looklab-cli card inspect | eval`.
- **Card inspector:** drop a `.llk` file; the `looklab-export` reader (wasm32) shows header fields, the META status, sections and content id. Rejections show the reference reader's first validation error; inspect again after fixing it. Local only.
- **Render-tag checker:** drop a JPEG; the client parses the `UserComment` render tag ([04 §8.4](04-matching-and-export.md)) and checks its `cid` against the published cards. A secondary paste field accepts raw render-tag text. Local only.
- **Listed firmware:** firmware ids seen in tagged renders from `GET /api/cards/verification`, with versions, looks verified and measured closeness. Empty state: "No firmware listed yet. Ship the render tag and your users' renders list it here." An unavailable server or server-less preview shows an unavailable state, not an empty list.

**Editing apps (`/developers/pipelines`):** what an editing-app pipeline declares ([04 §6](04-matching-and-export.md)), the published pipelines, and a link into the pipeline proposal (§4.3). The page uses "pipeline" throughout, so it no longer collides with photo-editing presets; the preset route lives on the custom-look form (§4.3).

## 9. Community

Heading "How the community decides", lede "Camera data changes through public proposals. Moderators check the evidence, and every decision is logged." The page explains, in plain words, how decisions are made: anyone can contribute; new contributors' data is capped until a trusted member vouches for them; moderators review changes to camera data; everything is logged publicly ([05](05-trust-and-integrity.md)). It shows recent activity as sentences built from the audit log ("Sunlit Negative 1.1 published", "X-M1 Classic Chrome mapping approved"), open proposals, a Propose button, a link to the full audit log with its JSON, and the moderator sign-in link (§6).

The current public API exposes individual proposals but no public proposal listing. The hub omits open proposals until a public list is available. Live activity reads `/log.json`; the server-less preview uses its published audit snapshot. Role grants and endorsements read the paginated public `/lists/` endpoints.

Recent activity uses display handles when supplied and role names otherwise. Proposal decisions link to the proposal, use its menu-level summary when supplied, and show the reason separately. Dates are local and abbreviated. Internal ids, sequence numbers and ISO timestamps appear only as secondary details in the full audit log.
